# Research: England Key Stage 1 (Year 1 and Year 2) as the level ladder

Purpose: define what the game should cover so a 5 year old can progress through
Year 1 (ages 5 to 6) and Year 2 (ages 6 to 7) content, and be stretched in depth
rather than skipped ahead.

How this was produced: a multi-agent research run (91 agents, 24 sources
fetched, 20 claims extracted, 16 confirmed by 3-vote adversarial verification,
4 refuted), followed by targeted searches to fill the gaps the run left. The
official gov.uk pages and White Rose Maths were blocked by this session's
network policy, so the programme of study wording below is taken from school
websites that reproduce the statutory text, cross-checked across several of
them. It matches the 2014 national curriculum, which is still in force. Treat
it as accurate but check gov.uk before quoting it anywhere official.

---

## 1. Status of assessment at the end of Key Stage 1

- End of KS1 tests (SATs) and teacher assessment became **non-statutory from
  2023/24**. The DfE still publishes the frameworks and optional papers and
  encourages schools to use them. For our purposes they remain the working
  definition of "expected" and "greater depth". (verified 3-0)
- The **Year 1 phonics screening check remains statutory**: 40 words read
  aloud one to one with the teacher, 20 real and 20 pseudo-words, pass mark
  32 out of 40, decoding only, no comprehension. A child who reads fluently
  at 5 will pass this without preparation; it tells us nothing useful for him.
- The 2025 Curriculum and Assessment Review left 2025/26 arrangements
  unchanged. A revised national curriculum is expected later; nothing below
  changes until then.

## 2. Mathematics, Year 1 programme of study

### Number and place value
- count to and across 100, forwards and backwards, beginning with 0 or 1, or
  from any given number
- count, read and write numbers to 100 in numerals; count in multiples of 2,
  5 and 10
- given a number, identify one more and one less
- identify and represent numbers using objects and pictorial representations
  including the number line, and use the language of: equal to, more than,
  less than (fewer), most, least
- read and write numbers from 1 to 20 in numerals and words

### Addition and subtraction
- read, write and interpret mathematical statements involving addition (+),
  subtraction (−) and equals (=) signs
- represent and use number bonds and related subtraction facts within 20
- add and subtract one-digit and two-digit numbers to 20, including 0
- solve one-step problems that involve addition and subtraction, using
  concrete objects and pictorial representations, and missing number problems
  such as 7 = ? − 9

### Multiplication and division
- solve one-step problems involving multiplication and division, by
  calculating the answer using concrete objects, pictorial representations
  and arrays with the support of the teacher
- (guidance) through grouping and sharing small quantities, begin to
  understand multiplication and division, doubling, and simple fractions

### Fractions
- recognise, find and name a half as one of two equal parts of an object,
  shape or quantity
- recognise, find and name a quarter as one of four equal parts of an object,
  shape or quantity

### Measurement
- compare, describe and solve practical problems for: lengths and heights
  (long/short, longer/shorter, tall/short, double/half); mass/weight
  (heavy/light, heavier than, lighter than); capacity and volume (full/empty,
  more than, less than, half, half full, quarter); time (quicker, slower,
  earlier, later)
- measure and begin to record lengths and heights, mass/weight, capacity and
  volume, time (hours, minutes, seconds)
- recognise and know the value of different denominations of coins and notes
- sequence events in chronological order using language such as before and
  after, next, first, today, yesterday, tomorrow, morning, afternoon, evening
- recognise and use language relating to dates, including days of the week,
  weeks, months and years
- tell the time to the hour and half past the hour and draw the hands on a
  clock face to show these times

### Geometry: properties of shapes
- recognise and name common 2-D shapes (rectangles including squares,
  circles, triangles) and 3-D shapes (cuboids including cubes, pyramids,
  spheres)

### Geometry: position and direction
- describe position, direction and movement, including whole, half, quarter
  and three-quarter turns

(No statistics in Year 1.)

## 3. Mathematics, Year 2 programme of study

### Number and place value
- count in steps of 2, 3 and 5 from 0, and in 10s from any number, forward
  and backward
- recognise the place value of each digit in a two-digit number (10s, 1s)
- identify, represent and estimate numbers using different representations,
  including the number line
- compare and order numbers from 0 up to 100; use <, > and = signs
- read and write numbers to at least 100 in numerals and in words
- use place value and number facts to solve problems

### Addition and subtraction
- solve problems with addition and subtraction using concrete objects and
  pictorial representations, including those involving numbers, quantities
  and measures, and applying increasing knowledge of mental and written
  methods
- recall and use addition and subtraction facts to 20 fluently, and derive
  and use related facts up to 100
- add and subtract numbers using concrete objects, pictorial representations,
  and mentally, including: a two-digit number and 1s; a two-digit number and
  10s; 2 two-digit numbers; adding 3 one-digit numbers
- show that addition of 2 numbers can be done in any order (commutative) and
  subtraction of 1 number from another cannot
- recognise and use the inverse relationship between addition and subtraction
  and use this to check calculations and solve missing number problems

### Multiplication and division
- recall and use multiplication and division facts for the 2, 5 and 10
  multiplication tables, including recognising odd and even numbers
- calculate mathematical statements for multiplication and division within
  the multiplication tables and write them using the ×, ÷ and = signs
- show that multiplication of 2 numbers can be done in any order and division
  of 1 number by another cannot
- solve problems involving multiplication and division, using materials,
  arrays, repeated addition, mental methods, and multiplication and division
  facts, including problems in contexts

### Fractions
- recognise, find, name and write fractions 1/3, 1/4, 2/4 and 3/4 of a
  length, shape, set of objects or quantity
- write simple fractions, for example 1/2 of 6 = 3, and recognise the
  equivalence of 2/4 and 1/2

### Measurement
- choose and use appropriate standard units to estimate and measure
  length/height (m/cm), mass (kg/g), temperature (°C), capacity (litres/ml)
  using rulers, scales, thermometers and measuring vessels
- compare and order lengths, mass, volume/capacity and record using >, < and =
- recognise and use symbols for pounds (£) and pence (p); combine amounts to
  make a particular value
- find different combinations of coins that equal the same amounts of money
- solve simple problems in a practical context involving addition and
  subtraction of money of the same unit, including giving change
- compare and sequence intervals of time
- tell and write the time to five minutes, including quarter past/to the hour
  and draw the hands on a clock face to show these times
- know the number of minutes in an hour and the number of hours in a day

### Geometry: properties of shapes
- identify and describe the properties of 2-D shapes, including the number of
  sides and line symmetry in a vertical line
- identify and describe the properties of 3-D shapes, including the number of
  edges, vertices and faces
- identify 2-D shapes on the surface of 3-D shapes (a circle on a cylinder, a
  triangle on a pyramid)
- compare and sort common 2-D and 3-D shapes and everyday objects

### Geometry: position and direction
- order and arrange combinations of mathematical objects in patterns and
  sequences
- use mathematical vocabulary to describe position, direction and movement,
  including movement in a straight line and distinguishing between rotation
  as a turn and in terms of right angles for quarter, half and three-quarter
  turns (clockwise and anti-clockwise)

### Statistics
- interpret and construct simple pictograms, tally charts, block diagrams and
  simple tables
- ask and answer simple questions by counting the number of objects in each
  category and sorting the categories by quantity
- ask and answer questions about totalling and comparing categorical data

## 4. End of Key Stage 1 maths standards (teacher assessment framework)

Three standards: working towards, working at the expected standard, working at
greater depth. Wording below follows the 2018 onwards framework as reproduced
by schools and the DfE exemplification; the refuted claim in the research run
was a mis-attribution between levels, not a wrong statement.

**Working towards** (selection)
- read and write numbers in numerals up to 100
- partition a two-digit number into tens and ones, demonstrating an
  understanding of place value, though may need apparatus
- add and subtract two-digit numbers and ones, and two-digit numbers and
  tens, where no regrouping is required
- recall at least four of the six number bonds for 10 and reason about
  associated facts
- count in twos, fives and tens from 0 and use this to solve problems
- know the value of different coins
- name some common 2-D and 3-D shapes from a group and describe some
  properties

**Working at the expected standard**
- partition any two-digit number into different combinations of tens and
  ones, explaining their thinking verbally, in pictures or using apparatus
- add 2 two-digit numbers within 100 (48 + 35) and subtract (72 − 17) using
  an efficient strategy, explaining the method
- recall all number bonds to and within 10 and use these to reason with and
  calculate bonds to and within 20, recognising other associated additive
  relationships
- recall multiplication and division facts for 2, 5 and 10 and use them to
  solve simple problems, demonstrating an understanding of commutativity
- identify 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 2/4, 3/4 of a number or shape, and know that all
  parts must be equal parts of the whole
- use different coins to make the same amount
- read scales in divisions of ones, twos, fives and tens
- read the time on a clock to the nearest 15 minutes
- name and describe properties of 2-D and 3-D shapes, including number of
  sides, vertices, edges, faces and lines of symmetry

**Working at greater depth**
- read scales where not all numbers on the scale are given and estimate
  points in between
- recall and use multiplication and division facts for 2, 5 and 10 and make
  deductions outside known multiplication facts (multiples of 5 end in 0 or
  5, so 18 × 5 cannot be 92)
- use reasoning about numbers and relationships to solve more complex
  problems and explain their thinking (29 + 17 = 15 + 4 + ?; "together Jack
  and Sam have £14, Jack has £2 more than Sam, how much does each have?")
- solve unfamiliar word problems that involve more than one step
- read the time on a clock to the nearest 5 minutes
- describe similarities and differences of 2-D and 3-D shapes, using their
  properties (two different 2-D shapes both have only one line of symmetry;
  a cube and a cuboid have the same number of edges, faces and vertices but
  different dimensions)

The pattern across all three greater depth frameworks is the same: greater
depth is **reasoning, explaining, deducing and multi-step**, not bigger numbers.

## 5. English, Years 1 and 2

### Reading comprehension (both years, Year 2 adds depth)
Year 1 pupils are taught to develop pleasure in reading, vocabulary and
understanding by:
- listening to and discussing a wide range of poems, stories and non-fiction
  at a level beyond that at which they can read independently
- being encouraged to link what they read or hear to their own experiences
- becoming very familiar with key stories, fairy stories and traditional
  tales, retelling them and considering their particular characteristics
- recognising and joining in with predictable phrases
- learning to appreciate rhymes and poems, and to recite some by heart
- discussing word meanings, linking new meanings to those already known

and understand books by:
- drawing on what they already know or on background information and
  vocabulary provided by the teacher
- checking that the text makes sense to them as they read and correcting
  inaccurate reading
- discussing the significance of the title and events
- making inferences on the basis of what is being said and done
- predicting what might happen on the basis of what has been read so far

Year 2 adds:
- discussing the sequence of events in books and how items of information
  are related
- becoming increasingly familiar with a wider range of stories, fairy stories
  and traditional tales
- being introduced to non-fiction books that are structured in different ways
- recognising simple recurring literary language in stories and poetry
- discussing and clarifying the meanings of words, linking new meanings to
  known vocabulary; discussing favourite words and phrases
- answering and asking questions
- explaining and discussing understanding of books, poems and other material

### The KS1 reading test's five comprehension strands (verified 3-0)
A ready-made skill taxonomy for the reading track:
- **1a** draw on knowledge of vocabulary to understand texts
- **1b** identify and explain key aspects of fiction and non-fiction texts,
  such as characters, events, titles and information
- **1c** identify and explain the sequence of events in texts
- **1d** make inferences from the text
- **1e** predict what might happen on the basis of what has been read so far

The test is comprehension only, two papers of 20 marks, Paper 1 roughly 400 to
700 words, Paper 2 roughly 800 to 1100 words, scaled score 100 = expected.

### End of KS1 reading standards (verified 3-0)
- **Expected**: read accurately most words of two or more syllables, most
  words with common suffixes, most common exception words; sound out
  unfamiliar words without undue hesitation; in a familiar book, check it
  makes sense, answer questions and make some inferences. About 90 words per
  minute is an indicator, not a threshold.
- **Greater depth**: in a book read independently, make inferences from what
  is said and done, make plausible predictions, and make links between the
  book being read and other books read.

### Writing, end of Year 2 expected (verified 3-0, paraphrase-level)
- write simple, coherent narratives about personal experiences and those of
  others (real or fictional)
- write about real events, recording these simply and clearly
- demarcate most sentences with capital letters and full stops, use question
  marks correctly when required
- use present and past tense mostly correctly and consistently
- use co-ordination (or, and, but) and some subordination (when, if, that,
  because)
- spell many common exception words; segment spoken words into phonemes and
  represent these by graphemes, spelling many correctly
- form capital letters and digits of the correct size, use spacing between
  words

### Grammar and punctuation (English appendix 2)
- **Year 1**: joining words and clauses using "and"; capital letters, full
  stops, question marks, exclamation marks; capital letters for names and
  for "I"; plural -s and -es; suffixes -ing, -ed, -er; prefix un-
- **Year 2**: subordination (when, if, that, because) and co-ordination (or,
  and, but); expanded noun phrases for description and specification (the
  blue butterfly, plain flour); statement, question, exclamation, command;
  present and past tense including progressive; commas in lists; apostrophes
  for contractions and singular possession; suffixes -ful, -less, -ly, -ness,
  -er, -est; compound nouns

## 6. How schools sequence it: White Rose Maths

White Rose is the most common scheme in English primaries. Term order (version
3, from Third Space Learning and school copies; the official site was blocked):

**Year 1**
- Autumn: place value within 10; addition and subtraction within 10; shape
- Spring: place value within 20; addition and subtraction within 20; place
  value within 50; length and height; mass and volume
- Summer: multiplication and division (counting in 2s, 5s, 10s, equal groups,
  doubling); fractions (halves, quarters); position and direction; place
  value within 100; money; time

**Year 2**
- Autumn: place value (to 100); addition and subtraction; shape
- Spring: money; multiplication and division; length and height; mass,
  capacity and temperature
- Summer: fractions; time; statistics; position and direction

Each block is broken into "small steps" of one lesson each. That is the right
granularity for a game level: one small step is one mini-game level.

## 7. Stretching a child who is ahead: depth, not acceleration

This was the strongest consensus in the research (verified 3-0 across NRICH,
NCETM/OUP mastery guidance 2015, ACME 2012):

- The national curriculum itself says pupils who grasp concepts rapidly
  "should be challenged through being offered rich and sophisticated problems
  before any acceleration through new content".
- Greater depth means: solving problems where the method is not obvious,
  exploring and investigating structure, generalising, conjecturing,
  explaining and justifying, all **within** the year's content.
- Acceleration is reserved for pupils with absolute mastery so far, very high
  assessment performance, social and emotional security, and a clear onward
  pathway. ACME warned it produces "shallow mastery" and often ends with
  children giving up maths.
- The mechanism NRICH recommends is the **low threshold, high ceiling** task:
  one problem every child can start, with a harder challenge hidden inside
  the same task. The canonical move is to change a parameter and ask for a
  generalisation: after checking a double-six domino set has 28 dominoes,
  extend to double-ten, double-twenty, and ask how many there must be and how
  you know the set is complete.
- Stage 1 NRICH examples: Sort the Street (sort houses in several ways),
  Noah (12 legs walked into the Ark, how many creatures, how many ways),
  Poly Plug Rectangles (find a hidden rectangle from its spot count). Stage 2
  for stretch: Magic Vs (1 to 5 in a V with equal arms), Number Differences,
  Reach 100 (four digits in a 2×2 grid making four two-digit numbers that
  total 100).

For a child who counts to 500, the implication is clear. Do not race to Year
3 numbers to 1000. Instead take Year 1 and Year 2 content and go deep:
"how many ways", "what if", "is it always true", "find all of them",
"explain to the hippo why".

## 8. What this means for the game: the level ladder

Each skill track gets a ladder of levels. Level numbers are per track, so he
can be at Year 2 greater depth in number and Year 1 autumn in shape.

### Number and place value
| Level | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | numbers to 10: count, one more/less, compare | Y1 autumn |
| 2 | numbers to 20: numerals and words, number line | Y1 spring |
| 3 | numbers to 50: tens and ones, count in 2s 5s 10s | Y1 spring |
| 4 | numbers to 100: count across 100, forwards and back from any number | Y1 summer |
| 5 | place value of each digit, partition 10s and 1s, <, >, = | Y2 autumn |
| 6 | count in 3s, in 10s from any number, estimate on a number line | Y2 autumn |
| 7 | partition any two-digit number in different ways (23 = 10 + 13) | KS1 expected |
| 8 | number riddles and reasoning: "bigger than 40, ends in 3, odd" | KS1 greater depth |
| 9 | depth: "find all two-digit numbers whose digits add to 7, how do you know you have them all" | LTHC stretch |

### Addition and subtraction
| Level | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | within 10 with objects, + − = signs | Y1 autumn |
| 2 | bonds to 10, related subtraction, missing numbers | Y1 autumn |
| 3 | within 20, bonds to 20, one-step word problems | Y1 spring |
| 4 | fluent facts to 20, derive facts to 100 (3 + 4 so 30 + 40) | Y2 autumn |
| 5 | two-digit plus/minus ones and tens, no regrouping | Y2 autumn |
| 6 | two two-digit numbers with regrouping (48 + 35, 72 − 17), three one-digit numbers | KS1 expected |
| 7 | inverse to check, missing number both sides (29 + 17 = 15 + 4 + ?) | KS1 greater depth |
| 8 | two-step unfamiliar word problems; "Jack has £2 more than Sam, together £14" | KS1 greater depth |
| 9 | depth: "three odd numbers always add to an odd number, show the hippo why" | LTHC stretch |

### Multiplication, division and fractions
| Level | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | equal groups, doubling, sharing with objects | Y1 summer |
| 2 | halves and quarters of shapes and small sets | Y1 summer |
| 3 | count in 2s 5s 10s to make arrays, odd and even | Y2 spring |
| 4 | 2, 5, 10 facts both ways, × ÷ = signs, commutativity | Y2 spring |
| 5 | 1/3, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4 of shapes and quantities; 2/4 = 1/2 | Y2 summer |
| 6 | deductions outside known facts (multiples of 5 end in 0 or 5) | KS1 greater depth |
| 7 | depth: "12 legs went into the Ark, how many animals, find every answer" | NRICH Noah |

### Measurement, money and time
| Level | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | compare long/short, heavy/light, full/empty, earlier/later | Y1 spring |
| 2 | coins and notes, days of the week, o'clock and half past | Y1 summer |
| 3 | cm and m, kg and g, litres, read a ruler and scale | Y2 spring |
| 4 | £ and p, make an amount with different coins, give change | Y2 spring |
| 5 | quarter past and to, time to five minutes, minutes in an hour | Y2 summer |
| 6 | read a scale with missing numbers, estimate between marks | KS1 greater depth |

### Shape, position and statistics
| Level | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | name circle, triangle, square, rectangle, cube, cuboid, sphere, pyramid, in any orientation | Y1 autumn |
| 2 | half, quarter, three-quarter turns; left, right, forwards, backwards | Y1 summer |
| 3 | sides, vertices, edges, faces, vertical line symmetry; 2-D faces on 3-D shapes | Y2 autumn |
| 4 | repeating patterns and sequences of shapes; clockwise and anti-clockwise | Y2 summer |
| 5 | tally charts, pictograms, block diagrams; total and compare categories | Y2 summer |
| 6 | "sort these shapes a different way", "two shapes with one line of symmetry that differ" | KS1 greater depth |

### Reading comprehension (strands 1a to 1e)
| Level | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | read a one-line instruction and act on it; match sentence to picture | Y1 |
| 2 | retrieval (1b): who, what, where from a 3 to 5 sentence text | Y1 |
| 3 | sequence (1c): order the pictures from a short story | Y1 / Y2 |
| 4 | vocabulary (1a): pick the picture that shows the word in context | Y2 |
| 5 | inference (1d): "the lemur put on his coat and scarf, what is the weather" | Y2 expected |
| 6 | prediction (1e): what happens next, then read on and check | Y2 expected |
| 7 | longer texts (100 to 300 words), two-step inference, link two texts | Y2 greater depth |

### Language and grammar (also feeds the three-language track)
| Level | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | join two sentences with "and"; fix the capital letter and full stop | Y1 |
| 2 | statement, question, exclamation, command: pick the right sign | Y2 |
| 3 | because, when, if, that: choose the joiner that makes the sentence true | Y2 |
| 4 | expanded noun phrases: build "the blue butterfly" from parts | Y2 |
| 5 | past and present tense: fix the silly sentence | Y2 |
| 6 | commas in a list, apostrophes in contractions | Y2 |

### Placement for him, day one
From what the parent has described: start number and place value at level 4,
addition at level 3, multiplication at level 1, measurement at level 2, shape
at level 1, reading at level 3, grammar at level 1. The adaptive system moves
each track from there within the first two sessions.
